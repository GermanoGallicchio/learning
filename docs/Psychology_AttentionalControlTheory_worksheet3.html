What each hypothesis predicts

Use this while mapping the studies. A study supports a hypothesis if its finding is the kind of thing that hypothesis predicts.

Hypothesis A study supports it if it shows… What that looks like
1 processing efficiency effort or resource use rising while performance holds or falls: effectiveness and efficiency coming apart effort ratings or pupil diameter rise under pressure while accuracy is unchanged
2 task demands the impairment being larger when the task is more demanding anxiety harms a complex version of a task more than a simple one
3 attentional control attention moving away from what the task requires, towards what is salient gaze leaves the target; the final fixation shortens under pressure
4 inhibition task-irrelevant or threatening information getting through that should have been kept out threat-related words slow responses; distractors capture attention
5 shifting difficulty moving attention to what the task now requires slow to re-engage with the task after an interruption
6 updating difficulty monitoring or refreshing what is held in working memory losing track of a changing score or sequence

Several do fit two. A study showing that threatening material captures attention supports hypothesis 3, because the balance has tipped towards the stimulus-driven system, and hypothesis 4, because inhibition failed to keep the material out.

Some studies measure the mechanism itself, such as gaze or effort. Others only measure performance and leave the mechanism to be inferred. A study that measures the mechanism is testing the hypothesis; a study that measures only performance is consistent with it. Say which kind each of your three is.

Attentional Control Theory: Sport

Two things could be targeted: where the performer looks before the next attempt, which acts on attentional control; or how the error is interpreted, which acts on the appraisal that ACTS says generates the anxiety. Choose one and say which mechanism it acts on.

Four design decisions

The study: test whether anxiety impairs shifting in a sport task. Choose one option in each row, and give one line of reasoning.

Decision Options
1. The task golf putting / reaction-time task on a screen / decision-making in a small-sided game
2. The anxiety manipulation competition with a leaderboard / an audience watching / a financial incentive
3. The measure of shifting time to return to the target after a distractor / accuracy when the cue changes part-way through / self-report questionnaire
4. The comparison high vs low trait anxiety, between participants / the same participants under low and high pressure

Worked example of the kind of answer wanted, using decision 1:

I would use the reaction-time task on a screen. Shifting is hard to isolate in a real sport task, where a performer might fail for many reasons at once, and the screen task lets me control exactly when the cue changes. The cost is that it is no longer sport: whatever I find might not transfer to a player on a pitch.

One or two sentences per decision, naming the cost as well as the choice.

Every option costs something. Changing your mind here is the point, not a mistake.
